~400MTier 3 — Z-class + towns+

"Tier 1/2/3" isn't one single official ranking in India — it's popular shorthand for the government's X/Y/Z classification (Department of Expenditure, used for HRA), which is what's shown here: X = Tier 1 (8 metros), Y = Tier 2 (~90 mid-size cities), Z = Tier 3 (everything else). Try before you scroll — practice prompts

1. Estimate the number of UPI transactions made for online food delivery in India per month.

Show a structuring hint
Start from UPI's ~698M daily transactions, isolate the share that's e-commerce/food-related, then apply the food-delivery segment's share of that basket — cross-check against Swiggy/Zomato's combined order volumes.

2. Estimate the number of EV two-wheelers that will be sold in Tier 2 & Tier 3 cities next year.

Show a structuring hint
Anchor on the 1.28M E-2W sold in 2025, apply a growth rate, then split by city tier — Tier 2/3 typically lags metro EV adoption, so weight metros higher before flipping the ratio for smaller cities.

3. Estimate the number of paying edtech subscribers in India for competitive exam prep (UPSC/CAT/JEE).

Show a structuring hint
Start from the 250M student base, narrow to the college/post-school segment likely to take competitive exams, apply a "preparing this year" rate, then a paying-subscriber conversion rate — sanity check against the $7.5B total edtech market.

4. Estimate the share of India's e-commerce market that comes from fashion & apparel.

Show a structuring hint
Use the $137B e-commerce market as the denominator; build the fashion share bottom-up from category buyers × average annual apparel spend, then compare the resulting % against your top-down guess.

Interview playbook

What interviewers are actually scoring, and the 60-second shape a strong answer takes.

S40%

Structure

Break into 2–4 MECE components before any number. Say it aloud. Draw a tree. They want to see your mind, not just your math.

A30%

Assumptions

State every assumption clearly. Good assumptions show business sense. Bad numbers with good logic still pass.

C20%

Communication

Think out loud. Don't go silent for two minutes. A confident wrong answer beats a silent right one.

N10%

Number sense

Being off by 30–50% is fine. 10x off is a red flag. Always sanity-check your final number.

Do this
Define before you calculateAlways state scope first: geography, time period, units. "I'll estimate this for India, for one year, in number of units."
State assumptions out loudEvery filter you apply — say it. "I'm assuming about 20% of internet users shop online = ~194M people." Shows business intuition.
Sanity check at the endBefore presenting, ask: does this feel right? Compare to a real-world reference, out loud — interviewers love this.
Use MECE structureMutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive. Split populations into non-overlapping groups: urban/rural, income tiers, age bands.
Don't do that
Jump straight to numbersStarting with "So India has 1.4B people, and 10% of them..." without any setup is the #1 red flag in guesstimate interviews.
Use obviously wrong anchors"Assume India's population is 500M" will derail your entire estimate. Know your five anchors cold before entering any interview.
Panic if your math is offIf you catch an error, calmly say "let me revise that." Self-correction shows maturity — defending a wrong number is far worse.
Over-engineer itA clean 3-step estimate beats a 10-step model that loses the interviewer. Precision is not the goal — a logical path to a reasonable ballpark is.
